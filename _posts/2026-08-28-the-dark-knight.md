It is almost boring to call *The Dark Knight* the best superhero movie ever made, because the statement has become a cliché. But clichés become clichés by being true, and this film earns every bit of its reputation. Christopher Nolan took a comic-book property and made a crime epic that happens to feature a man in a cape.

## What I liked

- **Heath Ledger.** The Joker here is not a clown; he is an argument. Ledger plays him as a force of nature that does not want money or power, only proof that anyone can be broken. It is terrifying because it is coherent.
- **The IMAX photography.** Nolan committed to large-format photography for entire sequences, and the result has a weight and clarity that digital intermediates still struggle to match.
- **The moral architecture.** Every set piece is also an ethical test. The ferry sequence is the obvious example, but even the opening bank heist is a lesson in mutual assured destruction.

## What could be better

- The third-act Rachel/Dawes love triangle resolution feels rushed, a casualty of the film's ambition.
- Some of the early-2000s CGI ages poorly in isolated shots, though far less than in comparable films of the era.

## Final thoughts

A blockbuster that trusts its audience to sit with discomfort. The Joker wins the argument; Batman just survives it. That tension is why the film endures.

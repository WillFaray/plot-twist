*Forrest Gump* is one of those films that people either embrace as a warm hug or reject as sentimental nonsense. I have landed in different camps at different ages, and on this rewatch I find myself somewhere in the middle: moved by its craft, occasionally irritated by its politics, and consistently impressed by Tom Hanks.

## What I liked

- **Tom Hanks.** He plays Forrest without a trace of condescension. The performance is technically precise — the posture, the gait, the vocal rhythm — but it never feels like a technical exercise.
- **The visual effects.** Placing a fictional character into archival footage was not new in 1994, but the execution here set a standard that still holds. The integration is seamless.
- **The structure.** The bench framing device gives the film a gentle, oral-history rhythm that makes its excesses easier to forgive.

## What could be better

- The film's treatment of historical trauma is superficial at best. Vietnam, Watergate, and the civil rights movement become backdrop for one man's luck, which is a choice that has not aged well.
- Jenny's character is more symbol than person, a problem the script never quite resolves.

## Final thoughts

It is a better film than its critics claim and a more problematic one than its admirers admit. The feather still floats, and I still watch it go.

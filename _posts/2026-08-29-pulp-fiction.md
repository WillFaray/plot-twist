Revisiting *Pulp Fiction* in 2026 is a strange experience. The film that once felt dangerous now feels like a masterclass in structure, which is both a compliment and a small loss. Quentin Tarantino's second feature remains one of the most influential movies of the 1990s, and its fingerprints are still visible on almost every nonlinear narrative that followed.

## What I liked

- **The dialogue.** Tarantino writes conversations that are about everything except what they are about. The "Royale with Cheese" exchange is famous for a reason: it builds character, rhythm, and worldview without advancing the plot by a single frame.
- **The structure.** The fractured timeline is not a gimmick; it is the argument. By reordering events, Tarantino turns a series of criminal mishaps into a meditation on chance and consequence.
- **The performances.** John Travolta, Samuel L. Jackson, and Uma Thurman all found career-defining roles here, and the smaller turns (Harvey Keitel's Winston Wolfe, Steve Buscemi's Buddy Holly waiter) are just as sharp.

## What could be better

- Some of the racial language has aged poorly, and not merely by contemporary standards; it was already gratuitous in 1994.
- The film's swagger can tip into self-congratulation, particularly in the longer diner sequences.

## Final thoughts

It is cooler and more controlled than it remembers being, which is the fate of all revolutionary art. Still, the Jack Rabbit Slim's dance scene alone justifies its place in the canon.

There are films that entertain, films that move, and then there are films that make you feel genuinely small against the scale of the universe. *Interstellar* belongs firmly in that third category. Christopher Nolan poured his obsession with physics, time, and the stubbornness of love into a movie that still provokes arguments almost twelve years later.

## What I liked

- **The sound design.** Hans Zimmer's organ-heavy score is not merely accompanying the images; it is *arguing* with them. The docking scene remains one of the most nerve-wracking things I have ever watched.
- **The practical work.** Nolan insists on real corn fields, real projections, miniatures where he can get away with it. The result has a texture that purely digital environments rarely achieve.
- **Matthew McConaughey.** He somehow makes astrophysics feel like a family argument, which is exactly the point of the film.

## What could be better

- The third-act exposition dump is clunky. An otherwise elegant metaphor gets spelled out in dialogue a beat too often.
- Some supporting characters on Earth are thinner than they deserve, sacrificed to the runtime.

## Final thoughts

It is imperfect, it is sentimental, it is sometimes scientifically loose. I do not care. When the tesseract sequence hits, every flaw shrinks to irrelevance. This is cinema as emotional physics, and it works.

If you have not watched *The Matrix* in the last five years, do yourself a
favor and revisit it on the biggest screen you can find. The film is
unbelievably dense: every frame hides a decision, a reference, a small
practical effect that still holds up twenty-five years later.

## What I liked

- **The pacing.** The Wachowskis commit to silence and stillness at exactly
  the right moments. The lobby scene is still one of the best action
  sequences ever filmed.
- **The world-building.** The film teaches you its rules in tiny,
  disposable conversations: hovercraft operators, the prophecy of "the
  One", the taste of steak. It never stops to explain.
- **Hugo Weaving.** A perfect, calm antagonist.

## What I think about today

Watching it again, the philosophy feels less like a novelty and more like
a *requirement* of the genre. The film argues, very seriously, that
choosing to live inside an illusion is still a choice — and that is
enough to make it feel uncomfortably current.

> "Unfortunately, no one can be told what the Matrix is. You have to see
> it for yourself."

## Verdict

Still electric. Still quotable. Still the smartest blockbuster of 1999.
